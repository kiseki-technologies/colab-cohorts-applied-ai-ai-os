# Interrogating research — the Week 2 prompts

"Summarise this" gets you a book report. Interrogation gets you insight.

**Do your own pass first.** Read the raw material and note what stands out before you prompt.
AI magnifies your judgement; it can't replace what you never formed.

---

## Themes, with evidence

```
You have [N] interview transcripts and the interview script in this project.

Identify the themes. For each one give me:
- how many participants raised it, and who
- verbatim quotes, attributed
- how strongly it was expressed — passing mention vs the thing they got animated about

Do not merge themes that sound similar but have different underlying causes.
Where a "theme" rests on one participant, label it as a signal, not a theme.
```

## Contradictions

```
Where do participants' stated preferences contradict their described behaviour?

Give me the quote for what they said and the quote for what they actually do.
These gaps are where the insight usually lives.
```

## Red herrings

```
What real pains did participants describe that are genuinely out of scope for [PRODUCT]?

I want to name them so we stop being pulled toward them. For each, say why it's out of scope
given [OUR THESIS].
```

## The one people forget

```
What did the interviewer miss?

Which answers should have been followed up and weren't? What should we probe next time?
Where did the script lead the participant?
```

## Findings vs insights

```
For each theme, separate the finding from the insight.

The finding is what we observed. The insight is why it happens and what it means for the
product. Where you can't get to the "why" from the evidence, say so rather than speculating.
```
